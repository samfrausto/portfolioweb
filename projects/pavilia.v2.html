Pavilia — Portal Mechanic

Two students, two worlds — and a shader that cuts a live window between them.

Role

Technical lead — portal system

Status

Completed

Engine

Unity

Stack

HLSL · Render Textures · C#

The problem

A window into another world can't be a picture of one.

My partner built a cherry-blossom tree and an island; I owned the visuals and the mechanic joining them. A portal fails the moment it feels like a flat texture — it has to behave like a hole in space: parallax as you walk past it, the other world tracking your exact perspective, and stepping through has to be seamless.

That's not an asset you download. It's camera math and shader work, and getting it right was the whole project.

The build

Two cameras, one illusion.

The portal works by keeping a second camera in the destination world that mirrors the player's position relative to the portal — then painting what it sees onto the portal surface every frame.

01

Dual-camera system

A second camera mathematically replicates the player's movement relative to the far portal, so the view through it is always perspective-true.

02

Render texture pipeline

The mirrored camera's output streams onto the portal surface as a live render texture — a real-time window, not a looping video.

03

Hand-tuned HLSL

The stock projection clipped the island's edges; I rewrote the shader so the full island reads through the portal from every angle.

04

Seamless teleport

Cross the plane and you're in the other world — the portal shuts off behind you with no pop, no fade, no seam.

What it proved

The mechanic shipped complete — and the shader work became the foundation for everything I now build in real-time graphics.
