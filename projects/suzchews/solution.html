§ 03.1

The gum, in concept.

What SuzChews is

A chewing gum. Formulated specifically for cancer patients losing their ability to taste during chemotherapy. Not a supplement. Not a drug. A gum - something a patient can reach for any time food becomes unrecognizable, without adding to their medication burden.

SuzChews is built around four active ingredient strategies - each designed to address one of the four taste-disrupting mechanisms chemotherapy produces in a patient's mouth. The strategies are designed to work as a system, not individually. Together, they form the flavor profile curve shown in Fig. 03.1 below.

01 · Trigeminal stimulation

Cooling, tingling, and carbonation effects (menthol, fizzing agents) activate the trigeminal nerve - which is anatomically separate from the gustatory (taste) nerve and unaffected by chemotherapy's attack on taste receptor cells. This pathway redirects sensory attention away from the metallic and bitter signal at the tongue, giving the patient a clean, recognizable sensation even when taste buds are compromised.

02 · Umami / peptide bitter masker

Savory peptides and amino acids compete at the bitter receptors against the reactive carbonyl compounds produced by lipid peroxidation. This competitive displacement reduces the actively-generated bad signal without synthetic pharma-grade bitter blockers - keeping the formulation food-grade and accessible.

03 · Sour / tart - first wave anchor

Sour-detecting taste cells are demonstrably less compromised under chemotherapy than sweet or salt receptors. Citric and malic acids front-load the chew with a familiar, strong flavor signal that cuts through the metallic background. For many patients, it is the first recognizable taste they experience in weeks.

04 · Anti-nausea support

Ginger, lemon, magnesium, and vitamin B6 in functional doses address nausea - the parallel barrier that prevents cancer patients from wanting to eat even when taste might otherwise permit it. This is not the primary mechanism of the gum, but it removes the barrier that makes the other three strategies moot if nausea wins.

Trigeminal vs gustatory

The trigeminal nerve carries chemesthesis (cooling, heat, tingle). It runs in parallel with but separate from the gustatory nerves. Chemotherapy damages taste receptor cells but leaves the trigeminal pathway largely intact.

Why not synthetic bitter blockers?

AMP-based bitter blockers are effective but not consumer-appropriate for an OTC gum. Umami peptides occupy a similar functional space with entirely food-grade ingredients at OTC-accessible price points.

For cancer patients specifically

Every formulation choice was made with the constraints of a patient on active treatment: no medication burden, accessible form factor, functional across nausea states, usable from day 4 through post-treatment recovery.

§ 03.3

Process innovation & stakeholders.

The process innovation in SuzChews isn't just the algorithm - it's the systematic approach to building something that can actually reach the people who need it. That means thinking through who is affected by this product, what they need from it, and how each group fits into the path from concept to patient.

The algorithm (§05) is the core process innovation: it replaces a fixed "average-patient" formula with a per-patient recommendation driven by symptom variables. But a product that only exists as an algorithm doesn't help anyone. The stakeholder structure below addresses how the product actually moves through the world.

Process innovation defined

The innovation here is in the formulation process itself - symptom-driven, variable, per-patient - not just the end product. A static SKU would serve average-case patients. The algorithm serves the actual distribution of patients.

§ 03.3.a

Who this affects.

Primary

Cancer patients on active treatment

Tasting food again. Reducing the mental load of eating during treatment. A product that works without adding to their medication burden.

OTC distribution via oncology clinic gift shops and care packages, positioned as a comfort product rather than a medical adjunct. No prescription required.

Primary

Family caregivers

Something concrete they can do. Watching someone they love not eat is one of the harder parts of being a caregiver - a product that helps, even partially, matters.

Caregiver-facing packaging and plain-language labeling. The product should be something a caregiver can hand over without needing to explain mechanism.

Secondary

Oncologists & oncology nurses

Patient adherence to treatment, nutritional status, and adjuncts that don't interfere with chemotherapy. They are cautious about recommending anything without evidence.

Food-product classification keeps SuzChews outside the clinical approval process while clinical use data accumulates. Early engagement through oncology dietitian networks.

Operational

Manufacturer & formulator

Stable formulation, supply chain, and margin. Specialty ingredient sourcing is a real constraint at this dose level.

Jel Sert internship provided direct R&D knowledge of functional confectionery manufacturing. Future: contract-manufacturer evaluation for specialty gum production.

Secondary

Patient advocacy (ACCO)

Childhood cancer patient quality of life and equitable access to supportive care products that go beyond standard clinical offerings.

ACCO patient packages as an aspirational distribution channel. The product's OTC, non-pharma positioning fits their model of patient-directed comfort products.

Regulatory

FDA / regulatory

Safety, claims substantiation, and the food-supplement-drug classification question.

Initial path: food product, no medical claims (functional gum category). If clinical data accumulates, evaluate medical food designation. Drug pathway deferred indefinitely.

§ 03.4

Who I am, and what made this.

Two of the most important people in my life went through cancer. Both of them were on chemotherapy. And the thing that stays with me - not the fear, not the waiting rooms, but the small thing - is watching them push food around a plate because everything tasted wrong. Like metal. Like nothing. Like something they couldn't describe.

That observation is where SuzChews starts. Not in the literature, not in a lab. In a kitchen, watching someone who used to love food stop wanting to eat.

I'm Samuel Frausto - a student at USC in ACAD 432 studying innovation design. Before this class, I interned at Jel Sert, a functional confectionery manufacturer whose products include Fla-Vor-Ice, Otter Pops, and Jamba-branded beverages. I spent time in their R&D environment working on product formulation, which is where I learned how flavor actually gets built - not as an afterthought but as an engineering problem with real tradeoffs.

That internship changed how I thought about the problem my family members had faced. It showed me that a functional gum isn't just a concept - it's a formulation target with a delivery mechanism, a flavor release curve, ingredient interaction constraints, and a manufacturing process. SuzChews is the result of applying that knowledge to a patient population that has nothing designed specifically for them.

What Jel Sert taught about formulation

The most important thing I took from the Jel Sert internship is that formulation requires a clear end goal. In a product R&D environment, you can't approach a formula by mixing ingredients together and seeing what works - you have to know exactly what the product is supposed to do before you select anything. The end goal defines every ingredient decision that follows it.

That insight directly shaped how SuzChews was designed. Rather than starting with a list of "things that might help dysgeusia" and iterating at random, the algorithm in §05 starts from a defined target: specific symptom profiles mapped to specific mechanisms, with each ingredient strategy serving an identified function. The formulation is systematic because the internship showed me that systematic is the only approach that actually produces a product designed for a purpose - not just a product that happened to come together.

The Jel Sert conversations were informal; the application to SuzChews is mine.
