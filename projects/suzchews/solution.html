§ 03.1

The gum, in concept.

SuzChews is built around four ingredient strategies — each chosen to target a specific dysgeusia mechanism identified in §02.2. Together they form the flavor profile curve shown in Fig. 03.1.

1 · Trigeminal stimulation

Cooling, tingling, and carbonation effects (menthol, capsaicin analogs, fizzing agents) activate the trigeminal nerve, redirecting attention away from the rancid/metallic/bitter signal generated at the tongue. The trigeminal pathway is anatomically separate from the gustatory pathway, which is why this works even when taste receptor cells are compromised.

2 · Umami / peptide compounds

Savory peptides and amino acids act as competitive distraction at the bitter receptors and as a positive overlay on whatever residual taste signal remains. Function as a "bitter masker" without using the synthetic bitter blockers found in pharma applications.

3 · Sour / tart stimulation

The first wave of flavor on the curve. Sour is one of the most robust primary tastes under dysgeusia — sour-detecting cells appear less compromised than sweet or salt receptors, and sourness sets the tone for everything that follows.

4 · Anti-nauseating support

Ginger, lemon, magnesium, and vitamin B6 in functional doses provide secondary nausea relief. This is not the primary purpose of the gum, but it removes a parallel barrier to the patient feeling like eating after chewing.

Trigeminal vs gustatory

The trigeminal nerve carries cooling/tingling/heat sensations — chemesthesis. It runs separately from the taste-specific (gustatory) nerves. Damage one, the other still works.

Why not synthetic bitter blockers?

Compounds like AMP-based bitter blockers are effective but not consumer-friendly for an OTC gum. Umami peptides occupy a similar functional space with food-grade ingredients.

How to read the model.

① Challenge → Product. The five mechanisms identified in §02.2 directly define the four ingredient strategies in the Product node. There is a one-to-many relationship: a single ingredient strategy may address two mechanisms simultaneously (e.g. trigeminal stimulation distracts from both the bitter signal from carbonyls and the metallic signal from metal-ion binding).

② Challenge → Process. The variability problem from §02.4 is what makes process innovation necessary. Without per-patient symptom decomposition, the gum is a single SKU with average-case efficacy. The mapping algorithm in §05 is the response.

③ Product ↔ Process. The flavor-profile curve in §3.1 is not a formulation; it is a template that the §05 algorithm parameterizes per patient. The Process node's outputs become the Product's specifications.

④ Process → Team. A solo project with a single executor places hard ceilings on what scope the Process can sustain. The decision to use a deterministic scenario picker in §06 (rather than a live LLM) is a direct consequence of this constraint — documented honestly in §3.4 and §06's methodology section.

⑤ Team → Challenge. The dashed motivational edge from Team back to Challenge is intentionally not removed: the project's founding observation — my parents — is what shapes the prioritization of which mechanisms get targeted first.

Why label the edges?

An unlabeled diagram is decoration. Labels turn it into a falsifiable claim: each edge can be defended or revised. This is the difference between a "systems model" and a flow chart.

§ 03.3.c

Societal sustainability.

Equity. Chemotherapy patients across income bands experience dysgeusia at similar rates, but access to mitigation strategies skews with insurance coverage and clinic resources. SuzChews is most valuable as an accessible intervention, which constrains pricing and channel decisions in ways a typical CPG product would not face.

Pediatric application. Childhood cancer patients experience dysgeusia on a longer recovery timeline relative to total expected life-years and against a background of developing food preferences. The ACCO partnership channel is therefore not a marketing afterthought but a primary distribution pathway under evaluation.

A note on gum bases

Conventional gum bases use synthetic polymers that are non-biodegradable. Natural-base alternatives (chicle, jelutong) exist and align with the equity/sustainability framing — trade-off is shelf life and cost.

§ 03.4

Solo project, distributed roles.

SuzChews is an independent, single-author project. There are no teammates. The full body of work — from literature review through formulation strategy through this website — is mine.

The class deliverable spec asks for "presentation of roles and skills of team members." For a solo project, the honest interpretation is to make the distribution of roles within a single person explicit, and to be transparent about the informal advisory inputs that shaped the work.

Informal advisory. The most substantial outside input came from conversations with R&D contacts at Jel Sert (where I previously worked as a product-formulation intern), drawing on knowledge of how functional confectionery is designed and manufactured. No formal collaboration agreement, no co-authorship; the formulation choices, ingredient strategy, and algorithm in this project are mine alone.

Earlier project framing (visible in the pitch deck linked in §07) referenced Dr. Emily Liman's lab at USC and the American Childhood Cancer Organization (ACCO) as prospective collaborators. Those were aspirational and were not formalized within the scope of this deliverable.

Why be explicit?

Honest reporting of what was and wasn't done is part of the methodology. Overstating collaboration is the kind of thing that makes work non-replicable.
