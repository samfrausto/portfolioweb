§07 - Process Innovation & Team SUZCHEWS / 2026.05 / 07

The process. The people. The why.

SuzChews is a solo project. But making it real requires a specific chain of partners - a formulation manufacturer, an advocacy organization, a regulatory body, and the clinical relationships to test it. This section covers the process innovation model that structures the work, the stakeholders who sit inside it, and the personal context that started the project in the first place.

§ 07.1

Process innovation.

SuzChews is not just a new product - it requires a new process for how a functional gum gets built. Traditional gum development starts with flavor first and adjusts from there. SuzChews inverts that: it starts from the patient's biomarker profile, maps that to a target ingredient mix, and only then determines flavor and texture around those functional constraints.

The five-step process below reflects how this project actually moved from observation to prototype. Each step feeds the next, and the §04 systems model shows how they connect across the full site.

Step 01
Observation

Personal contact with chemotherapy patients revealed a gap: no consumer product addressed taste distortion directly.

Step 02
Literature synthesis

Eight biomarker variables identified across peer-reviewed literature. CiTAS scale selected as the quantification method.

Step 03
Biomarker mapping

Variables mapped to dysgeusia mechanisms. Mechanisms mapped to ingredient strategies - the §03 pathway diagram.

Step 04
Ingredient design

Four ingredient strategies (trigeminal, umami, sour, anti-nausea) formulated against the mechanism map and the §02 flavor curve.

Step 05
Algorithm + prototype

Weighting algorithm built from the variable profiles. Physical prototype submitted to Jel Sert and ACCO for review.

§ 07.2

Stakeholder map.

Six groups sit inside the SuzChews ecosystem. Each has a distinct reason to care about the product and a specific role in moving it from prototype to distribution. The cards below identify what each stakeholder cares about and how the project engages them.

Primary user
Cancer patients

Restoring a sense of normalcy during treatment. Any product that makes eating feel less like a chore has outsized value during chemotherapy.

Patient simulation scenarios (§06) are authored from real clinical profiles to ensure the formulation actually maps to how dysgeusia presents in practice, not just in aggregate.

Support network
Caregivers & family

Finding something - anything - the patient will eat. Appetite loss is one of the most visible and distressing symptoms from a caregiver's perspective.

Product accessibility and simplicity are built into the design constraint. A gum is something a caregiver can hand to a patient without any clinical setup or instruction.

Clinical partner
Oncologists & the Liman Lab

Evidence-backed interventions. Symptom management that complements treatment rather than conflicting with it. Dr. Emily Liman's lab at USC contributed to the discovery of the sour taste bud cell, which directly underpins the §03 biomarker mapping.

All ingredient choices are food-grade and cross-referenced against known drug interactions. The CiTAS scale (from clinical literature) is used as the quantification method rather than anecdotal taste reports.

Manufacturing partner
The Jel Sert Company

Manufacturability. Functional ingredients need to survive the gum base process, maintain stability, and stay within food-grade parameters at scale. Jel Sert makes Otter Pops and other confectionery - they understand what is actually possible on a production line.

The prototype was submitted to Jel Sert contacts through a prior R&D internship relationship. The formulation was designed from the start with gum-base compatibility in mind, not after the fact.

Distribution + advocacy
ACCO

The American Childhood Cancer Organization focuses on improving quality of life for pediatric cancer patients. Taste distortion is particularly damaging for children during treatment, where maintaining appetite has direct developmental consequences.

ACCO is positioned as both a distribution channel and a validation partner. Their endorsement would provide both clinical credibility and access to the patient population most likely to benefit.

Regulatory body
FDA

Whether the product makes an implied health claim. A gum marketed to cancer patients sits in ambiguous territory between functional food and medical device, which affects the regulatory pathway significantly.

All ingredients are food-grade (GRAS status). Claims are positioned around taste support and palatability rather than treatment benefit, which keeps the product in the functional food category and avoids the clinical trial requirements of a drug or device.

Aspirational partnerships

Jel Sert and ACCO are named because they are the realistic next step from prototype, not because formal agreements exist. The Liman Lab relationship is based on published research from USC. These represent the intended partners if the project moves into production.

§ 07.3

Where this came from.

Both of my parents are cancer survivors. I watched each of them go through chemotherapy, and one of the most consistent and least-discussed parts of that experience was how much their appetite and taste changed. They knew they would feel sick. They didn't expect that their scrambled eggs would taste metallic, or that food they'd eaten their entire lives would suddenly be unrecognizable.

That observation stayed with me. When ACAD 432 at USC asked for a product innovation project, this was the obvious starting point. Not because it was easy, but because I already understood the problem from the inside - not just from a paper.

Academic context
ACAD 432 · USC

This project was built entirely within the Innovation and Design course at the University of Southern California. The course pushed for real research grounding and real stakeholder thinking, which is why the project reaches into clinical literature and production partnerships rather than stopping at a concept sketch.

Industry context
Jel Sert R&D internship

A prior internship in product formulation R&D at Jel Sert gave me a working understanding of how functional ingredients behave inside confectionery bases, what is manufactureable at scale, and what the actual production constraints look like. That background is the reason the formulation choices in §04 are grounded rather than speculative.

Research foundation
Liman Lab, USC

Dr. Emily Liman's lab at USC contributed to identifying the sour taste receptor cell (OTOP1), which is directly relevant to the §03 biomarker mapping. Having that research available at my home institution shaped which mechanisms the project treats as primary and which it treats as secondary.

Design philosophy
Food-grade by default

Every formulation decision was made under one constraint: it has to be food-grade. No pharma-grade bitter blockers, no novel compounds, no ingredients that require a prescription context. The goal was something a patient could pick up at a pharmacy without a doctor's recommendation. That constraint shaped the entire ingredient strategy.

I didn't start with a market gap. I started with two people I love eating food they couldn't taste.
Jel Sert products

Jel Sert makes Otter Pops, Wyler's Light, and other confectionery brands. Their production infrastructure and food science capability are the realistic manufacturing pathway for a product like SuzChews.
