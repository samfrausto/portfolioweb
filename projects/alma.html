Let the interaction carry the lesson

Two states from The Four Valves

Actual screenshots of two states in the same experience, not a before-and-after redesign.

The problem
Explain an unfamiliar subject to people without a cardiac background, without overwhelming them with text.
My role
Learning goals, lesson sequence, interaction design, visual direction, and review of AI-assisted implementation.

I wanted the interaction to carry the lesson. In The Four Valves, users switch between "The way in" and "The way out" to see which valves open and close. I directed the learning experience and used Claude Code to help implement the connected 3D model and controls.

One clear comparison. A two-position switch moves between filling and ejection, keeping attention on the two valve pairs.

Connected feedback. The model and open/shut labels change together, so the state is shown in both motion and words.

AI built in phases.
I reviewed each one.

When the AI initially tried to build everything at once, I restarted with a phased plan we created together. Before advancing, I checked whether each interaction matched my intent, fit the overall experience, and communicated the concept I wanted to teach. AI helped implement the work; I directed and reviewed each stage.

My review criteria

  • Does the interaction match what I had in mind?
  • Does it fit with the rest of the experience?
  • Does it communicate the concept I want to teach?

Less reading.
More room to explore.

I first noticed that the sections contained too much information. Watching one person use Alma, I then noticed their attention gravitate toward the buttons. That reinforced my decision to focus each section on its main idea and make the interaction carry the lesson.

A clearer beginning
I added an opening section with a title explaining what the viewer was looking at.
A lighter final section
I simplified the dense final section so the main point was easier to pick out.

This was an informal observation of one person, not a formal usability study or eye-tracking research.

A working experience

Alma connects a 3D heart, interactive controls, and diagrams in a browser-based learning experience. The Four Valves section is one example of the approach: let someone act, see what changes, and explore the relationship.

I reviewed the interactions against my teaching goals. Formal learning outcomes have not been measured.
