SuzChews · identity · attempt 4 · for approval

Three marks that weigh something.

The last three were rejected for having no mass, and the file confirms it — all of them were fill:none plus a 2.4px stroke, and all three drew a fungiform papilla, which is on your own rejected-subjects list. So this set inverts both. Every mark below is solid fill with zero strokes, and each one is a named object from the world of gum rather than a diagram of biology. The five-petal flower is not redrawn by eye: it is the chapter-04 flower at its exact proportions, in the relay's own yellow. Details at THE CONSTANT, at the bottom.

The three marks

Concept A
The Pellet
a single pillowed chew, flower struck into the face
40 / 24 / 16 px
one ink
reversed
The chew itself, at actual proportion. A coated pillow pellet is one of the few silhouettes that belongs to gum and to nothing else — it is not a pill (pills are capsules or flat discs) and not a mint tin. The flower is struck into the face the way a brand is struck into a real coated pellet, so the mark holds at 16px: at that size it is a warm rounded mass with a yellow rosette in it, and the rosette is still countable.
Watch — the pillow is the only one of the three that could be misread as a soap bar or a lozenge in isolation. It stops being ambiguous the moment the wordmark is next to it.
Concept B
The Gumball
one coated sphere, flower on the shell
40 / 24 / 16 px
one ink
reversed
The densest possible mark. A full disc is the maximum mass a 64-box can carry, and the gloss crescent plus the terminator shadow are what turn it from a dot into a sphere. It is also the literal unit the chapter-04 machine dispenses, and the flower sits on it exactly as it sits on the glass globe — the icon and the identity become the same object at two scales.
Watch — a filled circle with a rosette in it is the shape most likely to read as a generic badge. The gloss is doing the work; if you ever need a one-ink stamp, the gloss goes and it flattens.
Concept C · closest to the relay
The Machine
chapter 04, reduced to its silhouette
40 / 24 / 16 px
one ink
reversed
The least ambiguous of the three. Globe on a tapered pedestal is a shape nobody misreads — it says gum before the name does, which is the exact test the last three failed. It is also the only mark that is the same object as the relay chapter, so the portfolio and the case study end up quoting each other rather than merely matching colours.
Watch — it is the most complex, so it is the first to lose detail. At 16px the door and the collar close up and it becomes a ball on a wedge; still readable, but it is no longer carrying the flower. If a favicon matters more than a letterhead, that argues for A or B.

The lockup — machine above, name below

The mark stays exactly as approved — nothing struck into it, nothing shrunk to make room — and the name sits underneath. All three below are a single SVG, not an icon parked next to a text element, so the proportion between mark and wordmark is locked into the file and cannot drift when someone drops it into a layout. The wordmark is tracked to an exact width with optical letter-spacing; the glyphs are never stretched.

Lockup A · recommended
Matched
wordmark tracked to the width of the machine's foot
56 / 40 / 28 px wide
one ink
reversed
The name is exactly as wide as the machine stands. That shared edge is what makes a stacked lockup look drawn rather than assembled — the eye reads one object with a label, not two things that happen to be near each other. It is also the most compact of the three, which matters because a stacked lockup is always taller than it is wide.

Rejected — letters inside the mark

Kept as a record, not as options. All three put the name into the mark and all three cost something to do it: the seal buries the machine in a band, the glass label shrinks the flower, and the plate sets type too small to survive. The lockup above does the same job without paying any of that.

C-i · recommended
The Seal
letters get their own band around the machine
40 / 24 / 16 px
one ink
reversed
The letters stop competing with the object. Giving them a ring of their own means the machine keeps its full silhouette and the name still reads — and it turns the whole thing into one solid disc, which is the most mass any of these carry. The flower sits at six o'clock as the closing device, so it is still the constant. This is the only one of the three where the name is genuinely part of the mark rather than squeezed onto it.
Watch — below about 22px the band type goes to texture. That is normal for a seal; the fix is a second, lettersless size (plain C) for favicons, which you already have.
C-ii
The Glass Label
printed on the globe, under the flower
40 / 24 / 16 px
one ink
The most product-like of the three. This is how the name would actually appear on a machine — curved on the glass, flower above it. It keeps C's silhouette completely intact, so from across a room it still reads as the machine and nothing is lost.
Watch — the flower had to shrink to make room, which weakens the one element we agreed is fixed. It is also the first to fall apart small: by 24px the label is a smudge on the glass.

Keeps the flower at full size. The plate replaces the dispenser door rather than fighting it, so the base gets a purpose instead of an extra hole, and the globe is untouched — flower exactly as approved.
Watch — the pedestal is the narrowest part of the mark, so this is the smallest type of the three and the first to disappear. Realistically a large-format-only variant: packaging and letterhead, not a browser tab.

The constant — one flower, two places

the mark's flower
same geometry, one ink
petals
5, first at 12 o'clock, 72° apart — relay-engine.js for(pe=0;pe<5;pe++), pa2 = pe·1.2566 − 1.5708
petal / orbit
0.565 — relay .048 / .085. Petals meet edge-to-edge, they do not float.
core / orbit
0.259 — relay .022 / .085
yellow
#ECC74A — the relay's YELL=[236,199,74], tag 3, the only colour in the whole cloud set. Same hex, no re-picking.
The flower is the one element that is not up for discussion, so it is the one element that is measured rather than drawn. Whichever mark you pick, the rosette in it and the rosette on the relay's glass globe are the same shape at two sizes.

Side by side the diagnosis is visible rather than argued: the rejected mark is line, and it thins toward nothing as it shrinks. The new ones are area, and area survives — the 20px pair on the right is the whole case. Nothing in this set uses a stroke attribute, so there is no weight to lose.

My pick is C, with B as the working alternate. C is the only one that passes the test the last three failed — it says gum on sight, with no wordmark helping it — and it makes the case study and relay chapter 04 the same object rather than two things that happen to share a palette. B is what I would actually put in the favicon and the app icon, because a sphere is the densest, most reducible mark of the three. Those two are compatible: C for the lockup, B as the standalone glyph, same flower in both. A is the cleanest single object but it is the one I would have to defend.

What I did not do: no bitten disc, no pouch, no bubble, no mouth, no tongue or papillae — all previously rejected. No wrapper, no blister strip. The flower carries the reason the project exists and it is the only place that reason appears; there is no origin copy anywhere in this lockup, per the copy rule.

Every mark is a single flat SVG, no gradients, no strokes, no filters. Each renders correctly in one ink, reversed on dark, and at 16px — all three states are shown above rather than claimed. Tell me a letter and I will build out the chosen one properly: clear-space rule, minimum sizes, favicon set, and the header lockup wired into projects/suzchews/.
