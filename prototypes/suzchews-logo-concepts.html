Three marks for giving taste back.

Built in the case study's own palette, not the portfolio's. All three start from the fungiform papilla — the taste bud itself, the structure chemotherapy actually damages — so the mark is about the biology of the problem rather than the shape of the product.

Concept A
Papilla Bloom
40 / 24 / 16 px
The taste bud, whole. A receptor at full sensitivity, signal radiating in every direction. Reads as a bloom, a sun, a nerve ending. The amber core is the pore where flavor actually enters.
SuzChews
Concept B · carries the cancer story
Restored
40 / 24 / 16 px
The same bud, mid-recovery. One side blunted and grey — taste lost to treatment. The other reaching full and warm — taste coming back. The cancer is in the mark without a ribbon, without a cliché, and without making the product look like a medical device.
SuzChews
Concept C
Signal Return
40 / 24 / 16 px
Flavour coming back in. Arrows point inward — signal returning to a receptor that had gone quiet. Teal diagonals are the secondary pathways recovering. Most diagrammatic of the three, and the closest in spirit to your §-numbered figures.
SuzChews

Why the bud and not the gum. A chew shape says "confectionery." The papilla says what the product is for. It also survives being 16px in a browser tab, which a bitten lozenge does not.

My pick is B. A is the cleanest mark, but B is the only one that carries the reason the project exists — and it degrades to A's silhouette anyway if you ever need a simpler variant. C is the most literal diagram and probably belongs inside the case study as a figure rather than as the identity.

Every mark is a single flat SVG, no gradients, and works in one colour if it ever needs to be stamped or embroidered.
