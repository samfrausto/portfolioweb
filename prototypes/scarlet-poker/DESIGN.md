# Scarlet — first playable direction

User-approved: Texas Hold’em, multiplayer with a play-chip buy-in, bot practice, rich gold and scarlet inspired by USC. Scarlet is a provisional product name, not a USC affiliation.

Palette: background #300910, table #690F23, elevated surface #43101B, gold #E4BB64, ivory #FFF8E9, muted text #C7B5B8. Gold is used for actions, focus and active-seat cues, never as small text on ivory. Card suit ink is #AB193D or #241920.

Type: Georgia for the brand, heading and card ranks; system humanist sans-serif for player names and controls, with tabular numbers. No font downloads required.

Layout: entry screen balances an oversized serif invitation and a fan of cards against a compact form. Game opens onto a spacious oval table. Six fixed seat locations establish spatial memory; the current player sits at the bottom. Board and pot occupy the center. Actions sit in a stable bottom region. On phones the table becomes tall and narrower and the action controls stack.

Critical rules: explicit “Call 40” and “Raise to 120” labels; shared and private information stay distinct; no hover-only controls; focus and 44px controls throughout. Reduced motion disables card entry effects. Keep the playful card fan as the entry screen’s single expressive element, rather than adding casino ornamentation or generic feature cards.

Review against brief: deep scarlet and gold are deliberate user choices. Warm ivory belongs only to actual playing cards. No dashboard grid, gradients, avatar stock photos or ornamental statistics. Game state and the cards provide the hierarchy.

## Deck refinement

Use an original SVG deck with ivory paper, charcoal/scarlet suit ink, restrained antique-gold
rules, conventional number-card pip layouts and mirrored geometric court portraits. One renderer
serves entrance, table and help examples. Tiny opponent cards use a simplified large rank/suit
face at showdown so they remain readable. The guide lists ten concrete five-card examples and
keeps a visible Close action while its contents scroll. All artwork is generated vector code;
no third-party deck art or image generation was used.
